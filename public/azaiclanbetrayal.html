The Oda-Azai Betrayal
A Chain of Fire

The Azai clan betrayed Oda Nobunaga at the Battle of Anegawa in 1570, breaking an alliance that had united central Japan.

Quick Answers

In 1564 Oda Nobunaga bound his sister Oichi in marriage to Azai Nagamasa, turning a rivalry into an alliance that opened the road to Kyoto. Six years later Nagamasa broke that oath to honour an older loyalty — the Asakura clan — and the betrayal nearly destroyed Nobunaga. The war that followed at Anegawa and Odani erased the Azai house, but the bloodline survived in Chacha, who as Yodo-dono carried the feud into Osaka Castle itself.

What was the Oda–Azai alliance?

In 1564 Oda Nobunaga sealed an alliance with Azai Nagamasa by offering his younger sister Oichi in marriage. The pact shielded Nobunaga's northern flank and opened a secure corridor through Ōmi, allowing him to march on Kyoto and install Ashikaga Yoshiaki as shogun in 1568.

Why did Azai Nagamasa betray Oda Nobunaga?

In 1570 Nobunaga invaded Echizen to crush the Asakura clan. Nagamasa faced an impossible choice between his modern blood alliance with Nobunaga and his hereditary feudal loyalty to the Asakura. He chose the Asakura, attacking Nobunaga from behind and nearly annihilating the Oda army.

What was the Battle of Anegawa?

Enraged by the betrayal, Nobunaga combined forces with his ally Tokugawa Ieyasu. At the Anegawa River in 1570 the Oda–Tokugawa coalition routed the Azai–Asakura army, shattering their field supremacy and forcing the Azai to retreat to their mountain fortress at Odani.

How did the Azai clan fall?

In 1573 Nobunaga brought his full wrath upon Odani Castle. Realising the end had come, Azai Nagamasa committed ritual suicide, extinguishing the sovereign Azai line. His wife Oichi and their daughters were safely extracted — and daughter Chacha would later rule from Osaka Castle as Yodo-dono.

Yodo-dono bears Hideyoshi's first biological son, Tsurumatsu, at Yodo Castle. He is immediately named heir to the realm, cementing her power. However, his sudden death at age two shatters Hideyoshi's stability, upending the entire Toyotomi line of succession.

1595 Kyoto Bloodbath
The Sanjō-gawara Slaughter
To completely exterminate any rival claim to Hideyori, Hideyoshi orders the public execution of Hidetsugu's entire household at the Sanjō riverbed in Kyoto. Over 30 women, concubines, and infants are systematically decapitated. This horrific act permanently poisons the nation's loyalty to the Toyotomi.

The Purge of Kanpaku Hidetsugu
Desperate for a successor after Tsurumatsu's death, Hideyoshi adopts his nephew Hidetsugu and appoints him Kanpaku. Once Hideyori is born in 1593, Hidetsugu becomes an existential threat to the direct bloodline. Accused of treason, he is exiled to Mount Kōya and ordered to commit seppuku.
