The Oda-Azai Betrayal
A Chain of Fire

In 1570, during Nobunaga's invasion of Echizen, Azai Nagamasa broke from the Oda alliance, collapsing the pact that bound the two houses. This defection placed Nobunaga's Owari–Kyoto route in danger and forced him to withdraw via Wakasa and Fusakawa.

Quick Answers

In 1564 Oda Nobunaga bound his sister Oichi in marriage to Azai Nagamasa, turning a rivalry into an alliance that opened the road to Kyoto. Six years later Nagamasa broke from that alliance, facing a difficult choice between the Oda and his long-standing ties to the Asakura — a decision that placed Nobunaga's army in a pincer crisis and forced a withdrawal from Echizen. The war that followed at Anegawa and Odani extinguished the Azai house, but Nagamasa's line endured through his daughter Chacha, who as Yodo-dono carried the memory of both houses into Osaka Castle itself.

What was the Oda–Azai alliance?

In 1564 Oda Nobunaga sealed an alliance with Azai Nagamasa by offering his younger sister Oichi in marriage. The alliance made travel through Nobunaga's northern flank safer and made advancing toward Kyoto easier. In 1568 Nobunaga entered Kyoto with Ashikaga Yoshiaki under his protection, and Yoshiaki was invested as shogun.

Why did Azai Nagamasa betray Oda Nobunaga?

In 1570 Nobunaga invaded Echizen to crush the Asakura clan. Nagamasa faced a difficult choice between his new alliance with the Oda and his long-standing ties to the Asakura. Choosing to keep faith with Asakura, Azai forces threatened Nobunaga's line of retreat, and the Oda army was forced to withdraw hurriedly from Echizen.

What was the Battle of Anegawa?

Reacting to the Azai defection, Nobunaga joined forces with Tokugawa Ieyasu; in June 1570 the two sides fought at Anegawa. Defeated there, the Azai–Asakura army fell back to defending their own bases.

How did the Azai clan fall?

In 1573 Nobunaga besieged Odani Castle and Azai Nagamasa took his own life. Odani fell, and the Azai house was extinguished as a Sengoku daimyō house. Oichi and her three daughters were taken into Oda custody, and their eldest, Chacha, would later gain political standing at Osaka Castle as Yodo-dono.

The Death of Oichi
Following Nobunaga's assassination at Honnō-ji (1582), Oichi remarries Shibata Katsuie. When Toyotomi Hideyoshi storms their stronghold at Shizugatake in 1583, Oichi takes her own life alongside Katsuie, and her three daughters escape the castle and survive.

Chacha Joins the Toyotomi
The eldest daughter, orphaned by both parents, eventually came under the protection of Toyotomi Hideyoshi — the figure at the centre of the side that destroyed the Azai. The Legacy — Succession & the Fall of the Toyotomi
1589 – 1591 Fragile Lineage
Birth & Tragedy of Tsurumatsu
Yodo-dono bears Hideyoshi's first son, Tsurumatsu, at Yodo Castle. He is expected as the Toyotomi heir, and Yodo-dono secures a firm position within the regime. But Tsurumatsu dies young in 1591, leaving Hideyoshi once again without an heir.

1593 On Tour The Toyotomi Heir
Birth of Toyotomi Hideyori
Yodo-dono gives birth to Hideyori at Osaka Castle. Hideyori becomes the heir to the Toyotomi house, and after Hideyoshi's death he is its head, based at Osaka Castle. Through his mother Yodo-dono, Hideyori has Oda and Azai blood. The stage is set for a final familial tragedy.

1595 The Hidetsugu Executions
The Sanjō Riverbed Executions
In 1595 Hidetsugu, accused of treason, entered Mount Kōya and was ordered to take his own life. At the Sanjō riverbed, his wives, children, and attendants were executed. The event became a lasting symbol of the succession struggle and power structure within the Toyotomi regime.

1591 – 1595 On Tour The Toyotomi Succession Problem
The Purge of Kanpaku Hidetsugu
After Tsurumatsu's death, Hideyoshi, seeking an heir, adopted his nephew Hidetsugu and appointed him Kanpaku. But once Hideyori was born in 1593, a succession question arose over both Hidetsugu and Hideyori. Accused of treason, Hidetsugu entered Mount Kōya and was ordered to take his own life.

The Siege & Fall of Osaka
Tokugawa Ieyasu brings an end to the Toyotomi hegemony. In 1615 Osaka Castle fell, and flames swept the castle grounds. Accounts say Hideyori and his mother Yodo-dono took their own lives within the castle, though details of their final hours remain uncertain.
