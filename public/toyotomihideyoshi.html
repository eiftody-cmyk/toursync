Toyotomi Hideyoshi
Dew that falls, dew that fades — my life

Born in Nakamura in Owari Province, Toyotomi Hideyoshi rose as a retainer of Oda Nobunaga and eventually became the ruler of Japan. He began building Osaka Castle in 1583, establishing it as the political and military base of the Toyotomi regime.

Quick Answers

Born in Owari, Toyotomi Hideyoshi rose from a retainer of Oda Nobunaga to the ruler of Japan. As Kanpaku he stood at the highest pinnacle of the imperial court, while building a regime that unified the daimyō of the land. He built Osaka Castle and is known for a wide range of military operations combining sieges, blockades, castle-building and flooding. After Nobunaga was killed at Honnō-ji in 1582, he prevailed in the struggle for power and reached the summit.

How did Hideyoshi become Kanpaku?

Hideyoshi was born in Nakamura in Owari Province. His father is generally identified as Kinoshita Yasuke, but his status and origins are uncertain, and little is known in detail about his early genealogy. Serving Oda Nobunaga, he built up a record across military, diplomatic and administrative affairs and steadily rose in rank. The 1570 retreat from Kanegasaki is recorded in the Shinchō Kōki as leaving Kinoshita Tōkichirō in the castle as part of the rear guard, though sources differ on the composition of that rear guard and on Hideyoshi's precise role. After Honnō-ji in 1582 he won the Battle of Yamazaki and was appointed Kanpaku on 11 July 1585.

Why did Hideyoshi take the title Kanpaku rather than shogun?

Hideyoshi chose not the path of the shogun as military head, but the path of legitimizing his regime through the highest court offices of Kanpaku and Daijō-daijin. The claim that birth barred him from the shogunate is a simplification that current research does not treat as a historical inevitability. In 1585 he became the adopted son of Konoe Nobutada, took the name Fujiwara, and was appointed Kanpaku; the following year he received the surname Toyotomi and became Daijō-daijin. He reached the highest court position in a form unprecedented for one born to a warrior house.

Why was Hideyoshi famous for unusual siege tactics?

He is known less for frontal assaults than for a wide range of operations combining sieges, blockades, castle-building and flooding. The 1566 Sunomata building belongs to the later war-story episode of the "overnight castle" and cannot simply be taken as fact. In 1581 he tightly surrounded Tottori Castle and pressed the garrison by cutting off its provisions. In 1582 he flooded Bitchū Takamatsu Castle, diverting water from the Oda River to isolate it. Later records say a massive dike was raised in only twelve days, but recent research has examined the possibility that natural levees in the area were used. During the 1590 Odawara campaign he maintained the siege while holding tea gatherings and nō performances.

Why did Hideyoshi build Osaka Castle?

In 1583 Hideyoshi chose the heights of the Uemachi plateau — the area where Ishiyama Honganji is believed to have stood — and began building Osaka Castle. The grand black-and-gold castle was developed as a political and military base symbolizing the authority of the Toyotomi regime and Hideyoshi's standing as the ruler of Japan.

The Rise — From Owari Retainer to Trusted Captain (1537–1581)
c. 1537 On Tour Owari Origins
Born in Nakamura, Owari
Hideyoshi was born in Nakamura in Owari Province. His father is generally identified as Kinoshita Yasuke, but his status and origins are uncertain, and little can be stated with confidence about his childhood. The year 1537 is now treated as the most likely birth year, though accounts of the circumstances of his birth vary.

1550s On Tour Adolescent Mythos
Wandering and the Sandal-Warming Tale
Little is known with certainty about Hideyoshi's youth. Later war stories record him wandering the provinces as a pedlar and stable hand, and the famous tale of warming his master's frozen sandals in his own kimono is one of the Hideyoshi legends that took shape after his death. Almost nothing about these years can be confirmed from contemporary sources.

1560 Oda Retainer
His Place in the Oda Clan
Contemporary sources for Hideyoshi's precise role around the Battle of Okethazama are limited. As Nobunaga extended his power from Owari into Mino, Hideyoshi too gradually took on more important duties within the Oda house.

1561 Domestic Anchor
Marriage to Nene
Around 1561, Kinoshita Tōkichirō married Nene (later Kitsunomiya / Kōdai-in). Nene is traditionally identified as a daughter of Sugihara, adopted by Asano Nagakatsu, and the genealogy of her family also carries uncertainties in later records. Their relationship, which began before Hideyoshi became a daimyō, continued alongside the making of the Toyotomi house.

1566 On Tour Prefabricated Warfare
The Sunomata Castle Legend
Later war stories tell of Hideyoshi building a castle at Sunomata in a very short time — the "overnight castle" episode. The tale of floating timber rafts downriver by night to raise a complete fort at once is famous, but it must be handled with caution before being taken as fact. The Sunomata building has to be considered together with both its practical reality and its legend.

1570 On Tour Rear Guard Crisis
The Kanegasaki Escape
The Shinchō Kōki records that during the retreat from Kanegasaki in 1570, Kinoshita Tōkichirō was left in Kanegasaki Castle as one of the rear guard. Sources differ, however, on the composition of the rear guard and on Hideyoshi's precise role.

1577 – 1581 Chūgoku Campaign
The Blockade of Tottori
Commanding the campaign against the Mōri in the west, Hideyoshi avoided bloody frontal clashes. In 1581 he surrounded Tottori Castle tightly and pressed the garrison by cutting off its provisions; as provisions ran low within the castle, it was steadily cornered.

The Rise to Power — Building a Regime after Nobunaga (1582–1585)
1582 Hydraulic Siege
The Flooding of Bitchū Takamatsu
Hideyoshi flooded Bitchū Takamatsu Castle, diverting water from the Oda River to isolate the castle. Later records say the great dike rose in only twelve days, but recent research has examined the possibility that natural levees in the area played an important role.

1582 (June) On Tour The Miracle March
The Battle of Yamazaki
On learning of Honnō-ji, Hideyoshi concluded a hurried peace with the Mōri and made the "Chūgoku Ōkaeshi" — the rapid return from Bitchū. Marching some 200 km, he hurried to the Kyoto area and defeated Akechi Mitsuhide at Yamazaki on 13 June. The victory rapidly raised Hideyoshi's political standing in the contest to succeed Nobunaga.

1583 On Tour Regime Base
Osaka Castle Construction Begins
Hideyoshi began building Osaka Castle on the strategic heights of the Uemachi plateau, the area where Ishiyama Honganji is believed to have stood. Its black-lacquered keep was thickly clad in gold leaf, and the grand castle was developed as a political and military base symbolizing the authority of the Toyotomi regime and Hideyoshi's standing as the ruler of Japan.

1583 (Spring) On Tour The Succession Dispute
The Rout at Shizugatake
Opposing Shibata Katsuie and others in the struggle over Nobunaga's succession and the leadership of the Oda regime, Hideyoshi fought the decisive battle at Shizugatake in April 1583. Katsuie withdrew to Fujimatsu Castle and took his own life together with Oichi. The victory removed one of the largest military forces standing in the way of Hideyoshi's regime.

1585 On Tour Imperial Co-optation
Appointed Kanpaku (1585)
Hideyoshi chose not the path of the shogun as military head, but the path of legitimizing his regime through the highest court office. In 1585 he became the adopted son of Konoe Nobutada, took the name Fujiwara, and was appointed Kanpaku on 11 July — an appointment unprecedented for one born to a warrior house.

The Age of Hideyoshi — The Toyotomi Regime and National Unification (1586–1591)
1586 The Toyotomi Name
Granted the Toyotomi Surname & Made Daijō-daijin
In 1586 Hideyoshi received the surname Toyotomi and became Daijō-daijin. Standing at the top of the court as Kanpaku while holding the highest office in the warrior world, he put the institutions of the Toyotomi regime in place.

1587 Western Expansion
The Kyūshū Campaign
In 1587 Hideyoshi sent a great army to Kyūshū and brought the Shimazu clan to submission. Shimazu Yoshihisa submitted to Hideyoshi, and the placement of daimyō and control of land across Kyūshū were reorganized under the Toyotomi regime. Kyūshū was brought within the orbit of Toyotomi rule.

1590 The Final Stand
The Grand Siege of Odawara
Hideyoshi surrounded Odawara Castle with a large army while building fortifications around it and holding tea gatherings and nō performances. The long siege isolated the Hōjō politically and militarily, and in July Ujinao surrendered.
Click to expand
1590 On Tour Architectural Illusion
Ishigakiyama Ichiya Castle — The "Overnight Castle" Legend
On Ishigakiyama, overlooking Odawara, Hideyoshi built a hilltop castle of stacked stone to serve as his headquarters. It came to be called the "overnight castle" because, after building the castle within the forest, the surrounding trees were all cut away at once, making it appear as if the castle had sprung up in a single night. The actual construction is thought to have taken about 80 days.

1591 Pax Toyotomi
Total Unification Achieved
Through the Odawara campaign and the Ōshū Assize of 1590 and the re-Ōshū Assize of 1591, Hideyoshi's unification of the country was completed. Generations of civil war were brought to an end, and Hideyoshi — born of humble origins — became the ruler who brought the daimyō of the land into submission.

1580s – 1590s Propaganda & Noh
History and Legend around Hideyoshi
Under Hideyoshi's regime, many documents and war stories were produced to record and celebrate military achievements and the unification of the country. Ōmura Yūko's Tenshōki is one such work. Its accounts often praise Hideyoshi's deeds, making it an important source for thinking about his self-presentation and the historical consciousness of the Toyotomi regime. Records also survive of large-scale tea gatherings such as the Kitano Ōchanoyu of 1587 (sources give varying attendance figures, from around 800 to over 1,000).

The Twilight — Continental Invasion and the Succession Problem (1592–1598)
1592 – 1598 The Era of Continental Invasion
The Imjin War Invasions
Planning an advance into Ming China, Hideyoshi launched a military invasion of the Korean Peninsula in 1592. He never crossed the sea himself, and from his base at Hizen Nagoya Castle in Kyūshū he continued to issue detailed and hardline instructions on the course of the war.

1593 On Tour Dynastic Bloodline
Birth of Direct Heir Hideyori
In 1593 Toyotomi Hideyori was born at Osaka Castle to Yodo-dono (Chacha). Hidetsugu already held an important position in the contest over the kanpaku office and the Toyotomi succession, but Hideyori's birth changed those relationships decisively. In 1595 Hidetsugu was removed from the kanpaku office, exiled to Kōyasan and then took his own life, while his wives and children were also executed.

September 18, 1598 On Tour The Final Dewdrop
Death of Toyotomi Hideyoshi
In his final years his illness grew serious, and he died at Fushimi Castle on 18 August 1598 (18 September in the Gregorian calendar), at the age of 61. Hideyoshi's death poem — "Dew that falls, dew that fades — my life; the affairs of Naniwa are a dream within a dream" — sets both life and the glory of the realm against dew and dreams. His death left a five-year-old heir, Hideyori, and the fate of the Toyotomi house rested on the child's shoulders.

Continue the Story at Osaka Castle

Hideyoshi's death poem reduced even a ruler's glory to "a dream within a dream." Seventeen years after his death, Osaka Castle burned in the Summer Siege of Osaka, and the Toyotomi house fell. What Hideyoshi left behind was not only a vast castle: the succession system centered on the young Hideyori, a political order that brought the daimyō of the land together, and a distinctive governing framework linking the imperial court and the warrior government. The one who would finally decide its fate was the young heir left in Osaka Castle, Toyotomi Hideyori.
